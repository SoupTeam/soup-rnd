Problem: isolated_compare previously computed a fresh 1800s deadline after the
outer 1800s supervisor had already started. The outer process group could be
killed while an inner supervisor still owned a separate arm process group.

Launch contract:
- Compute D once BEFORE starting the outer supervisor. D must be no later than
  launch_time + 1800s and must respect the remaining paid lease and export reserve.
- Pass exactly D to both setup_supervisor --deadline-unix-s and the pinned script's
  isolated_compare --deadline-unix-s. Retain outer --max-seconds 1800.
- CUDA mode refuses a missing, nonfinite, expired or insufficient-reserve deadline
  before creating output, importing calibration or spawning processes.
- All inner arm supervisors receive W=min(D, controller_start+1800)-40 seconds.
  The 40s gap reserves time for inner process-group cleanup and result persistence.
  Per-arm 300s bound remains. Never recalculate or extend W after calibration.
- Refuse another arm when W expires. Preserve BLOCKED/TIMEOUT evidence; no retry.
- Calibration and its observer inherit the outer process group (no new session).
  They remain covered by the outer deadline even if direct I/O stalls.
- CPU smoke defaults to a 180s bound with the same 40s reserve, or accepts an
  explicit outer deadline for nested local tests.

The caller must implement the shared-D contract; the CLI cannot discover another
process's deadline. This is bounded cleanup under normal scheduler/clock behavior,
not a guarantee against host failure, clock discontinuity or uninterruptible I/O.
Existing clocks/ownership controls and evidence review remain mandatory.
